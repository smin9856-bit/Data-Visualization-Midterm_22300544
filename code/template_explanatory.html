Richer, but less CO₂: __N_DEC__ of __N_HI__ high-income countries already did it, even counting imports. South Korea did not.

A common belief is that a growing economy has to burn more carbon. Data from the mid-2000s to the late 2010s shows that most rich countries have already broken this link, and that it is not just a result of moving factories abroad.

For: Korean university students and general readers who follow climate and economy news · Data: Our World in Data (Global Carbon Budget), Gapminder · All charts use fixed settings, nothing needs to be clicked.

Step 1. Look at the typical rich country. We set each country's 2004–06 average to 100 and took the median across the __N_HI__ high-income countries for every year. After the mid-2000s the two lines open up like scissors: GDP per person keeps rising, while CO₂ per person (consumption-based, so goods imported from other countries are counted too) goes down. Korea's lines stay together much longer.

Step 3. Did rich countries just "export" their emissions? If they did, the CO₂ fall would disappear once imports are counted. The hollow dot is CO₂ produced inside the country, the filled dot includes imports. For the decoupled countries the filled dots stay clearly below zero, about as low as the hollow dots. Korea is on the other side of zero in both measures.

What this does and doesn't show

  • This is about per-person change. Total national emissions can look different when population grows.
  • It shows that growth and CO₂ can move apart. It does not show why (energy mix, industry structure, policy, ...), so no causal claim is made here.
  • Being in the "decoupled" corner does not mean emissions fell fast enough for climate targets.
  • Consumption-based CO₂ is a model estimate (input-output tables) and exists for 120 countries only. The end period stops at 2019 to avoid the COVID dip. Using 2020–22 or single years gives 27–30 countries, and starting from 1999–01 gives 25 of 42.
  • Income groups are the current World Bank groups, not the ones of 2005.
