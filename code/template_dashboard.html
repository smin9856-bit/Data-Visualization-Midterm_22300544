Growth vs. Carbon Explorer (1990–2022)

Do countries that get richer always emit more CO₂? Explore income and CO₂ per person for 195 countries, and check whether a country's emissions moved together with its economy.

Who is this for

Students and general readers who are interested in climate and economy news, and want to check claims like "growth needs more emissions" with real data by themselves.

Questions you can explore

  • In a given year, how strongly is income related to CO₂ per person? Which countries are far above or below the overall pattern?
  • Did my selected country's CO₂ per person go down while its GDP per person kept going up?
  • Is a country's consumption-based CO₂ (including imported goods) higher than its production-based CO₂?
  • Did the low-carbon share of electricity change around the time emissions changed?
  • Who are the top emitters per person in each region, and did the ranking change between 1990 and 2022?

How to use

  1. Year slider: changes the year of the scatter plot, the top-20 ranking and the numbers at the top. The dashed line in the right charts marks this year.
  2. CO₂ basis: switch between production-based and consumption-based CO₂ for the scatter plot and the ranking.
  3. Country: pick from the dropdown, or click a circle in the scatter plot. The selected country gets a black ring, and the three charts on the right show its history.
  4. Region: click a region in the legend to keep only that region (shift + click to add more, double-click the legend to clear).
  5. Hover on any mark to see exact values.
  6. Press Reset view to go back to the default (South Korea, 2019, production-based, all regions).
Default view: South Korea · 2019 · production-based CO₂ · all regions
Data. CO₂ per person (production and consumption-based) and population: Our World in Data CO₂ dataset (based on Global Carbon Budget), github.com/owid/co2-data. Low-carbon share of electricity: Our World in Data energy dataset (Ember / Energy Institute), github.com/owid/energy-data. GDP per person (constant 2021 international $, PPP) and region/income groups: Gapminder Fast Track, gapminder.org/data (GitHub). Accessed 2026-10-22.
Notes. Production-based = emissions inside the country's borders. Consumption-based = production − emissions in exports + emissions in imports; it exists for only 120 of the 195 countries, so countries without it disappear from the scatter and the ranking when that basis is selected (they are not set to 0). Regions follow Gapminder's 4 regions, so Oceania (e.g. Australia, New Zealand) is counted as Asia. Income groups are the World Bank groups provided by Gapminder (current classification, not the one of each year). Missing years are shown as gaps, not as zero.
